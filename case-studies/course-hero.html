Designing How Students Study Across a Semester

8 min read | Course Hero (2024–2025) · Staff Product Designer · Course Experience Team

Course Hero was great at answering a single question in the moment, but students needed help getting ready for what came next: a quiz, a midterm, a final. I designed two generations of AI-native study products, Study Tools and Study Boards, both anchored to a student's own course rather than a generic library. I validated both with KANO surveys and painted-door tests before a line of code was written.

Context

Key Finding

Students struggle to turn their course content into prep materials before milestones like tests and exams. Preparation and course specificity surfaced as a massive unmet need.

Course Hero had built its business on being a great resource for students in their homework help. A student stuck on a specific homework problem could search on Google or Course Hero directly and find a solution from a massive library of student-uploaded documents. It worked at scale (1.8 million unique visitors a day), but the relationship was transactional. Students needed more — a semester-long study companion that could help them prepare for upcoming milestones like tests and exams using their own course materials.

Students were already going elsewhere to prepare (study groups, Quizlet, Anki) because Course Hero had no tools for active practice. We ran fast, cheap validation through KANO-model surveys against common study feature sets (flashcard generation, practice-question generation, AI "teach me" explanations, source comparisons) to separate must-haves from delighters from features students were indifferent to. That prioritization is what put the practice-question generator and flashcard generator ahead of other candidates.

We paired that with painted-door and smoke tests: real entry points placed in the live product, measuring actual click-through and intent. It validated that practice questions and flashcards "created from your own course material," rather than generic library content, was the version worth building.

Key Insights

Research Foundation

User interviews and diary studies, paired with KANO surveys and painted-door/smoke tests.

Students needed to prepare, and getting unstuck in the moment wasn't enough: it was the biggest unmet need in the market

Study Tools was scoped around practice and active recall: flashcards and practice tests, rather than more answers

Generic study material didn't feel trustworthy; it had to be the student's own, from their actual school, course, and professor

Every practice question and flashcard set generates from the student's own upload, URL, or pasted text, the same premise that gated Course-Add to a real school and course

Course Hero already had the documents needed to power a semester-long product; it just had no structure to use them

Study Boards auto-populates a course from content already on file the moment a student adds their school and course

Trust in AI output was complicated; some students distrusted it outright, others trusted it blindly

Designed for both ends: visible sourcing and explanations for the skeptical, explicit hallucination disclaimers for the overconfident

Phase 1

Study Tools

An AI flashcard generator and AI practice-question generator built from a student's own course material: upload, URL, or pasted text in, multiple question formats out (multiple choice, free response, true/false, matching). Every result included explanations, source citations, and a side-by-side comparison to the original material.

A "Learn Mode" added matching, flashcard review, and an AI "Teach Me" conversational mode.

Study Tools introduced the concept of Course Hero being an avenue for course study, something it hadn't been before. These offerings competed directly with products students were already using to address this need (Quizlet, QuizGecko, Magic Notes, etc.).

The Next Challenge

Even with good tools, Course Hero had no "place" for a student to return to. Every session started cold, and the tools built for Study Tools had no real home in the existing IA either. Course Hero had no concept of "your course" or "your semester"; every visit started from zero.

Phase 2

Course-Add

The moment a student added their school and course, Study Boards populated itself from existing assignments, labs, tests, and exams, with zero additional upload effort.

Phase 3

Study Boards

A course-level workspace: add your school and course, and Study Boards auto-populates from existing assignments, labs, tests, and exams already on file for that course, with no manual upload required.

From that base, it generates a semester-long study guide, flashcards organized by topic and theory, mock tests and exams a student can actually take, and a condensed cram sheet for last-minute review.

Add your own course and study materials to make it even better and tailored to your study needs.

Phase 4

Dashboard & Navigation Overhaul

Giving Study Space a real home required structural changes beyond the feature itself: a redesigned course-add flow, a dashboard reorganized around a student's active courses rather than past searches, and navigation changes to make the new semester-long surface discoverable rather than buried under search.

My Role & Impact

I joined as staff designer for the Course Experience pod, designing two generations of AI-native study products end to end: Study Tools and Study Space, including the trust and disclosure patterns for AI output and the navigation, dashboard, and course-add redesign needed to give Study Space a real home. I ran the KANO surveys and painted-door tests that validated both products before a line of code was written, partnering closely with PM, engineering, UX research, data science, and the AI/ML team throughout.

Impact

Made pre-build validation the norm

Ran KANO surveys and painted-door tests before either generation had a line of code. By the second round, that was just how the team approached AI investment.

Built in a reason to keep getting better

Both generations pulled from a student's own course, not a generic library, so every upload made that course a little richer for the next student who added it. Study Boards and Study Tools kept improving from ordinary use.

Trust and disclosure patterns spread beyond Study Tools

Other teams picked up the same visible-sourcing and hallucination-disclosure approach for surfacing AI content elsewhere in Course Hero.

Reflection

Shipping Study Tools early made the case for Study Space, before it would've been easy to call that version done. Validation didn't stop at launch, and giving the work a real home mattered as much as any single feature.
